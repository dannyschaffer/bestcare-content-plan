The call

We are not going to win head terms, and we should stop wanting to. Every high-volume query in this space — “car insurance”, “household insurance”, “best car insurance South Africa” — is held by direct insurers with domain authority in the 33–64 range and years of link equity. bestcare.co.za is a fifteen-year-old domain with no current content and effectively no live link profile.

What is genuinely open is the explainer layer. Across the clusters below there are roughly 13,000 monthly searches sitting at SEO difficulty under 35, mostly phrased as questions, and mostly answered today by thin product pages rather than by anyone actually explaining the thing. A broker is the natural author of that content in a way an insurer is not.

Google clicks are the slow curve; LLM citation is the fast one. Judged on organic traffic alone the ceiling here is modest — the best informational post we found in SA motor insurance is iWyze’s retail-vs-market-value explainer at roughly 412 estimated visits a month, and Auto & General’s blog posts land between 21 and 63 visits each. But being quoted in an AI answer doesn’t require position one. It requires being in the retrieved set with the most extractable answer on the page, and on difficulty-13 South African questions that is reachable in weeks rather than quarters. That is why this plan is written LLM-first, and why we measure citation before we measure clicks.

The three highest-value openings are unclaimed. Vehicle valuation (retail vs market vs trade), e-hailing cover for Uber and Bolt drivers, and what to do when a claim is rejected. All three sit at SEO difficulty 13–15, all three convert to a broker conversation, and Best Care has nothing on any of them.

The LLM visibility play

Anton’s instinct is right, with one important split. “Showing up in ChatGPT” is not one thing — it is three mechanics running on different clocks, and they respond to completely different work. Content wins the first outright. It barely touches the second. The third is a long game nobody can yet prove.

Mechanic one

Cited as the source

Someone asks an assistant a question. It retrieves a handful of pages and quotes the one that answers most cleanly, with a link. This is winnable with content alone, it does not need position one, and it is where all 24 posts in this plan are aimed.

Mechanic three

Known before the search

A model answers from what it absorbed in training rather than looking anything up. Common Crawl — 2.3 billion pages a month, free and open — is the corpus most models are assumed to draw on. Being in it is cheap. Proving it moved anything is not.

The reputation half, and why it outranks our blog

Stephen Burns and James Dooley made the same point from the practitioner side in their recent session on LLM ranking factors, and Dooley put it first on his list: what drives brand mentions is consensus of reputation — awards, reviews and testimonials syndicated across third-party sites, and case studies that tie your entity to other named entities. His argument is that this has to be echoed across the internet rather than asserted on your own website, because a model weighing two candidates takes the one other sources corroborate. The tracked account above arrived at the same conclusion independently, which is about as good as evidence gets in this field.

That reframes something I got too cautious about in the first version of this plan. Dooley’s payoff for a strong reputation base is comparison and “best of” content — and I ruled that out on FAIS grounds. The distinction that resolves it: Best Care should not publish rankings of insurers, but Best Care should absolutely be featured in other people’s rankings of brokers. Being listed in a third-party “best insurance brokers on the West Rand” piece carries none of the regulatory weight of publishing one, and it is worth considerably more to mechanic two. The work is outreach, not writing.

One tactic from that conversation we are not taking. Dooley suggests buying an industry award if you don’t have one — “whoever buys the largest table at the award ceremony seems to win.” That may be survivable in the agency world. For an authorised FSP, paying for a credibility marker that consumers reasonably read as independent is a reputational and plausibly a regulatory problem, and it is exactly the kind of thing that ages badly. Entering real awards on merit is fine and worth doing — the FIA’s annual intermediary awards are the obvious South African candidate, and someone should confirm the current categories and entry dates.

What this changes about how we write

Every post runs through the /blog-article-hybrid skill rather than being drafted freehand. That format already does most of what citation rewards: a quick-answer block in the first 100 words, section headings phrased as questions where they map to a real query, each section self-contained enough to be lifted out of context and still make sense, a comparison table, an FAQ accordion with matching FAQPage schema, and an E-E-A-T author bio. We’re using the hybrid variant specifically so the articles still read like articles instead of one long FAQ — an assistant will happily quote prose, and a human reader won’t stay for a wall of questions.

Three additions on top of the skill’s defaults, all specific to this client. Every factual claim gets a named, dated South African source, because a model choosing between two answers takes the attributable one. Every article states plainly that Best Care is an intermediary and not an insurer, so the entity is described correctly when it is summarised. And the rand figures, FSP number and office details must be phrased identically everywhere they appear — inconsistent entity details are the most common reason a model hedges or omits a small brand.

Technical pre-flight

Burns’ headline warning is that crawler blocking is the quiet killer — he puts it at over 35% of all websites, including major chains that are pouring budget into AI visibility while their own CDN turns the crawlers away, unaware. So this is worth checking properly rather than assuming.

✓

No blocking in the repo. netlify.toml contains a build block and one cache header for fingerprinted assets. No WAF rules, no bot filtering, no user-agent gates. robots.txt is Allow: / with no AI-crawler exclusions, so GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, CCBot and Google-Extended all have access.

□

Still to confirm in the Netlify dashboard. Bot protection and firewall settings are account-level and never appear in the repo, so a clean netlify.toml does not prove we’re open. Someone needs to look at the dashboard once the domain is pointed and the site is actually serving.

✓

Rendering is already right. CCBot crawls plain text only — it does not execute JavaScript and does not see images or video. The site is prerendered and a no-JavaScript check passed during the launch audit, so the article body is in the served HTML rather than behind a hydration step. This matters for the hybrid format too: infographics carry no weight with these crawlers, so anything an image conveys must also exist as text.

✓

llms.txt fixed. It described Best Care as a “proposed South African short-term insurance brokerage website” — a launch-draft artefact sitting in the one file written specifically to tell models what this business is. It now opens with the real entity and carries a business identity block: legal name, registration number, FSP 8754, office, phone and email, so the canonical facts are in one machine-readable place.

Stale pre-launch copy still on the site. FSP 8754 is live in the site content and in the FinancialService schema, but several pages still say the FSP details “must be verified before publication” and the About page still describes the brokerage in the future tense. On Common Crawl and harmonic centrality

The most interesting idea in that session is also the least proven, so it gets a proportionate amount of our attention. CCBot doesn’t rank by PageRank; it uses harmonic centrality — how close your domain sits to the web’s trusted core, weighted by inverse distance. Burns’ analogy: PageRank asks how many roads lead to your house, harmonic centrality asks how close you are to the centre of the city. The practical consequence is that one link from a site near the core moves you more than a pile of mid-tier links, which is a genuinely different way to buy links.

Dooley is running a 200-domain experiment to find out whether any of this reaches current models, and he is candid that after ten weeks it is inconclusive and he expects six to eight months before it tells him anything. Burns confirms Common Crawl keeps no logs and cannot say who downloads the corpus, and that the labs filter it heavily. So we are not building a strategy on this. What we are doing is using it as a free tiebreaker: when we do go after links for Best Care — directories, local business associations, industry bodies — check harmonic centrality alongside the usual authority score. A DR 10 site close to the core may be worth more than a DR 30 one that isn’t, and almost nobody selling links prices that in yet. Free checkers at audit.stephenburns.ai and the raw graph at webgraph.crawl.org.

One thing to do before week 1

The research point from that session is the cheapest win on this page: before publishing, look at what kind of page these models actually extract for our queries. In the agency niche it turned out to be pricing pages, review and testimonial pages, and directory listings — not blog posts. If South African insurance queries pull mostly from insurer product pages, the ombud, and comparison sites, that tells us where to aim and what not to waste three posts a week on. An hour of running our ten prompts and reading the citations, before the first draft, is worth more than any single article in this plan.

Compliance guardrails

These are the rules the writing team works inside, not suggestions. Insurance is financially consequential content and Best Care is a licensed intermediary, not an insurer. Anton sees every post before it publishes.

✕

We do not rank or compare named insurers. That means walking away from roughly 5,200 searches a month — “best car insurance South Africa” (1,900/mo), “cheap car insurance South Africa” (1,600/mo), “top 10 cheapest car insurance in South Africa” (720/mo) and the rest of that family. A licensed FSP publishing a ranked list of insurers invites a fair-clear-and-not-misleading problem, and the site’s own audit already commits to not claiming universal market access. Our version of that intent is the existing How to compare insurance policies guide, which teaches the comparison without doing it.

✕

No premium figures presented as quotes. Cost articles use dated, sourced ranges and say plainly that the only real number comes from a quote. Every figure carries its source and the month it was accurate.

—

“May cover”, never “will cover”. No article promises a claims outcome, states that a specific loss is covered, or implies a policy wording we haven’t seen. Cover descriptions stay general and point to the schedule.

—

Every post carries the FSP number and a link to the FAIS disclosure. No longer blocked — Best Care Insurance Brokers CC, registration 1992/100995/23, Authorised FSP 8754 is live in the site content and in the FinancialService schema. Use that exact wording in every byline and disclosure so the entity reads identically everywhere.

—

Named author and reviewer on every article. The AEO audit flagged missing bylines as a live trust gap. We need one named, qualified reviewer — most sensibly Stacey — attached to each post before it goes live.

—

No data capture inside articles. Readers route to the existing quote and contact forms. Nothing new collects personal information until the POPIA sign-off on the policy-change form is closed out.

How we’ll know it’s working

Because this is an LLM-first plan, the headline metric is citation, not clicks. The automated tracked-prompt slots on the Ubersuggest account are committed to another client, so Best Care gets measured the manual way: one person, ten fixed prompts, run on the first Monday of each month against ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, with the answer and any cited sources pasted into a sheet. It takes about twenty minutes. Because the prompts and the date are fixed, month-on-month movement is still real movement, and the cited-sources column doubles as ongoing competitor research — we find out who is being quoted instead of us.

Roughly two-thirds of the set mirrors the anchor posts, so we can see content converting into citations, and one-third is commercial, so we can see whether the brand is entering shortlists at all. Run these verbatim every month; changing the wording resets the baseline:
